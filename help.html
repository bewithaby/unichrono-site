Getting started

Two minutes from install to a live widget on your home screen.

  1. Open the app. Your own city is added for you on first launch, so the list is never empty. A short tour explains the main ideas; you can replay it any time from Settings → How to use.
  2. Add the places you care about. Tap the + button, search by city or country, and tap a result. There are 6,312 cities to choose from, ranked so the big ones come first.
  3. Rename them to suit you. Tap a city, then rename it: Mom, NYC office, Standup. Your name is what shows up in the app and in the notification.
  4. Put a widget on the home screen. Open Settings → Widget gallery, swipe through the five styles, and tap Add to home screen. Or long-press an empty spot on your home screen, open Widgets, and find Unichrono there.
  5. Set that widget up. The setup screen opens as soon as the widget lands. Pick its cities, format and colours, then Save. You are done: the widget keeps itself correct from here on.

What you need: Android 8.0 or newer for the app and four of the five widgets. The Analog Clock widget needs Android 12 or newer, because only from that version can Android give an analog face its own timezone.

Your city list

The main screen. One card per place, live, in whatever order you want.

What each card shows

  • Your label for the city, with its country flag before it (flags can be turned off in Settings).
  • The current time there, in 12 or 24 hour form, with seconds if you switch them on.
  • A part-of-day icon: morning (5am–10am), day (11am–4pm), evening (5pm–8pm) or night (9pm–4am). The bands are fixed local hours, so the icon is the same on every phone.
  • The context line: the zone abbreviation and UTC offset (for example IST · UTC+5:30), how far ahead or behind your own phone it is, and whether that city is on Yesterday, Today or Tomorrow relative to you.

Managing the list

Add

Tap +, then search by city or country name. Every entry carries its own IANA timezone, so daylight saving is handled for you, forever.

Reorder

Press and hold the grip handle on a card and drag it. The order you set is the order used in the app, the notification and any new widget.

Remove

Swipe a card sideways to delete it. Widgets already on your home screen keep their own city list and are not affected.

Rename

Tap a card to open its details, then the pencil. Clear the field to go back to the original city name.

City details

Tap any city to open its detail sheet.

The sheet gathers everything about that place in one view:

  • Country and time zone, with the current UTC offset.
  • The local date in that city right now.
  • Sunrise and sunset, calculated from the city's coordinates. Tap the date to check any other day, which is how you find out whether it will still be light there when your call ends. Far north or south, where the sun does not rise or set at all, the sheet says Sun up all day or Sun down all day instead of inventing a time.
  • Currency and international phone code, handy before a call or a transfer.
  • Alarm and Meeting buttons, which open the planner already anchored to that city.
  • The pencil, to give the city your own name.

Time travel

Answer "what time is it there when it is 4pm here" without doing any arithmetic.

  1. Open the ruler. Tap Time travel on the main screen. A scrub ruler docks at the bottom.
  2. Slide it. Every card re-bases to that moment at once, including the day labels, so you can see who is already on tomorrow.
  3. Jump to another date with Pick date if the meeting is not today.
  4. Act on the moment. The chips on the ruler hand the selected instant straight to Set alarm or Schedule meeting.
  5. Close it to snap every card back to now.

Nothing you do in time travel changes your list or your widgets. It is a view, not an edit.

Alarms and meetings

The two icons at the top of the main screen open one shared board: every city, at one instant.

How the board works

  • Every city you keep is listed at the same moment in time. Change the time in any row and all the other rows follow, so you can think in whichever city you think in.
  • The row you edit becomes the anchor and is badged as such.
  • Pick the time with the scroll wheel or the clock dial; Switch to dial and Switch to wheel toggle between them and the app remembers your choice.
  • Set date moves the whole board to another day.
  • + Add a city to check pulls in a place that is not in your list, just for this board. It disappears when you close the sheet.
  • Availability dots mark who the slot lands badly for, so you can see the 6am victim before you send the invite.

Alarm mode

Pick the time in any city and tap Set alarm. Your phone's own clock app gets the matching local time, so the alarm rings at the right real-world moment.

Why the alarm button sometimes greys out. Android's alarm handoff accepts an hour and a minute but no date, so the alarm always means "the next time that hour comes round". When the moment you picked would land on the wrong day, Unichrono disables the button rather than set an alarm you cannot trust. Use a calendar event or a city alert for those.

Meeting mode

  • Add to calendar opens your calendar app with a new event pre-filled at that instant.
  • Share invite builds a text block listing the time in every city on the board and hands it to the share sheet, ready for chat or email.
  • Alert me in this city turns the slot into a city alert (see below).
  • Label is optional and rides along into the event and the shared text.

City alerts

Be told when a chosen time arrives in another city. The bell icon in the top bar.

  1. Tap Add alert and choose a city, from your own list or from the full search.
  2. Pick the time in that city's local clock, and give it a label such as Standup if you want one.
  3. Choose when it repeats: once on a date, every day, or on the weekdays you select (for example Mon, Wed, Fri).
  4. Choose how it arrives: Notification only is a silent notification; Speaks aloud adds a spoken line and a buzz.
  5. Save. The alert row shows the city, the time there, the repeat, and what that works out to in your own time.

Alerts are rescheduled automatically after a reboot, and one that was missed while the phone was off is handled sensibly rather than firing at random.

Two permissions matter here. Notifications must be allowed, or an alert has no way to reach you; the screen offers a shortcut to that setting. If exact alarms are not allowed, the screen says Alerts may arrive a few minutes late and offers Allow exact alarms.

When your phone is silent, a spoken alert only vibrates; in Do Not Disturb it stays fully quiet. Unichrono never talks over a silenced phone.

Speak the time

Settings → Speak the time. Your phone's own local time, said out loud, hands-free.

  • Hourly announces on the hour, inside a window you set: Start announcing at is the first hour of the day it may speak, and Stop announcing at is the hour from which it stays quiet. Set both to the same time to announce all day.
  • Daily announces once, at the one time you choose.
  • Try it plays a sample immediately so you can hear the voice before you commit.
  • Announcements respect the ringer: silenced means vibrate only, Do Not Disturb means silence.

This feature speaks your own device time. To be told about a time in another city, use city alerts instead.

Widgets: the basics

The main reason the app exists. Five styles, all of them live.

Why they never freeze

Most world clock widgets redraw the time from a background job, which battery optimisation, Doze and OEM task killers eventually shut down. That is why competing widgets stop at some stale time.

Unichrono runs nothing in the background to keep time. Each clock ticks straight from the system clock with its own timezone attached, so it stays live and correct with no process of ours running, and it costs no battery.

Adding one

  1. From the app: Settings → Widget gallery. Swipe through the styles, each with a live preview, then tap Add to home screen.
  2. From the launcher: long-press an empty part of your home screen, open Widgets, and scroll to Unichrono. The order there matches the gallery.
  3. Set it up. The setup screen opens automatically. Whatever you choose applies to that one widget only.

Changing a widget later

Tap the widget itself. That opens its setup screen again, with its current cities and colours ready to edit. To change its size, long-press it on the home screen and drag the handles. More space means more rows and more detail per row; less space means the widget shrinks its text and, if it must, shows a +N more footer rather than clipping a city.

Removing one

Drag the widget to Remove as usual. Its settings are cleaned up with it; your city list in the app is untouched.

Editing a city row

Each city in a setup screen has a menu on the right with every action spelled out: Rename gives that row your own label, Change city swaps the place while keeping its position, and Remove drops it. Widgets that hold exactly one city, such as Analog and each half of Two Cities, offer the first two only.

Names on widgets

A widget snapshots the city names as they were when you set it up, so renaming a city in the app later does not silently rewrite a widget you were happy with. To change a widget's labels, tap it and edit them there.

Long city names

Names are shortened intelligently rather than chopped, so two cities on a small widget never end up looking identical.

Clocks in your notifications

See every city's time from inside any other app.

  1. Turn on Clocks in notification in Settings. On Android 13 and newer you will be asked for notification permission once.
  2. Pull down the shade from anywhere and your cities are there, ticking live, using your own labels and your chosen time format.
  3. Tap it to jump into the app.
  4. Swipe it away when you are done. It stays gone until you switch it on again, so dismissing it is respected rather than undone.

The notification is optional and off by default. Like the widgets, it ticks from the system clock, so it costs nothing to leave on.

The Quick Settings tile

Unichrono also ships a Quick Settings tile. Pull the shade down fully, edit your tiles, and drag Unichrono in for a fast glance and a one-tap way back into the app.

Keeping it reliable

Settings → Protect the notification. Only needed on phones that fight their own apps.

The widgets and the notification work without any setup on most phones. Some manufacturers, though, aggressively restrict apps in the background, and that can remove the ongoing notification or stop it refreshing after a reboot. If you see that, allow Unichrono to run in the background. The app stays light either way: the clocks tick by themselves without using battery.

The exact wording differs by phone. Look for one of these:

  • Settings → Apps → Special app access → Background power management (or consumption management) → Unichrono → Don't restrict.
  • Battery settings for Unichrono → choose Unrestricted.
  • Battery optimisation list → find Unichrono → Don't optimise.

The Protect the notification screen in the app detects your phone's flavour and opens the right settings page for you where it can.

Troubleshooting

The questions that actually come up.

My widget shows the wrong time

Unichrono reads your phone's clock and applies each city's timezone rules from the database Android ships. If every city is off by the same amount, the phone's own clock is wrong: turn on Set time automatically in your phone's date and time settings. If one city looks wrong right around a daylight saving change, check that your phone has the latest system update, since the timezone rules come with it.

The launcher says "Couldn't add widget"

Some launchers refuse a widget that is dropped onto a spot too small for it. Free up a bigger area on the home screen and try again, or add it from Settings → Widget gallery instead, which asks the launcher to place it for you.

How do I change a widget I already placed?

Tap the widget. Its setup screen opens with the current settings. To resize, long-press it and drag the handles. Nothing about a placed widget requires deleting and re-adding it.

My widget lost a city, or shows "+2 more"

It ran out of room. The widget shrinks its text first and only then hides the overflow, so the cities you do see are never clipped. Make the widget wider or taller, or switch its row detail to Compact to fit more rows in the same space.

The clocks notification disappeared

Almost always the phone's battery manager removed it. See Keeping it reliable. Re-enabling Clocks in notification brings it back immediately.

My alerts arrive a few minutes late

Exact alarms are not permitted for the app. Open the alerts screen and tap Allow exact alarms; the banner disappears once the permission is granted.

An alert stayed silent

Check that notifications are allowed for Unichrono, and that your phone is not silenced or in Do Not Disturb. Spoken alerts deliberately only vibrate when the ringer is off, and stay fully quiet in Do Not Disturb.

The "Set alarm" button is greyed out

Android's alarm handoff carries an hour and a minute but no date, so it always means the next time that hour comes round. When the moment you picked would ring on the wrong day, Unichrono disables the button instead of setting a misleading alarm. Add a calendar event or a city alert for that slot.

Renaming a city did not change my widget

That is intentional. Widgets keep their own labels so an in-app rename cannot rewrite a widget you already set up. Tap the widget to edit its labels there.

I cannot find my city

Search by country too, and try the nearest large city. Any city in the same timezone shows exactly the same time, and you can rename it to whatever you like afterwards. If a place you need is genuinely missing, use Settings → Request a feature.

The Analog Clock widget is not available on my phone

It needs Android 12 or newer. Below that, Android cannot give an analog face its own timezone, so it could only ever show your local time. The other four widgets work from Android 8.0.

Privacy and data

There is nothing to leak.

  • Unichrono has no network permission. It cannot send anything anywhere, and the city data ships inside the app rather than being fetched.
  • No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking SDKs.
  • Your cities, labels, widget settings and alerts are stored on the device only.
  • The permissions it does ask for are the obvious ones: notifications for alerts and the clocks notification, alarms for alert timing, vibration, and a boot signal so your alerts survive a restart.
  • City data comes from GeoNames.org, licensed CC BY 4.0.

Full details are in the privacy policy.
